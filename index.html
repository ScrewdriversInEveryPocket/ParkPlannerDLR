          Sources and methods

          Average wait time comes from Thrill Data's by‑date history (thrill-data.com): for each calendar date, the mean posted wait across every attraction that reported, averaged over 2023 to now. Their per‑date history starts in 2019, but only the 2023‑onward figure is used here, so the COVID closure and reopening years are left out.

          Crowd rating comes from Queue‑Times' daily crowd calendar (queue-times.com) for 2015–2019 and 2023 through Oct 23, 2026, about 6,400 park days. 2020–2022 are left out because of the COVID closure and the reservation and reduced‑capacity period after it. It is a rank, not a headcount: each day is compared with every day the park has been tracked since late 2014. Because some years run busier than others, each year's average is removed before averaging (so a quiet week in a busy year still counts as quiet), the result is put back on the 2023–2026 scale, and 2023–2026 days count double, since the older years predate Galaxy's Edge, Lightning Lane and today's annual passes. Read the rating for its shape (which weeks and days are lower) rather than its level.

          Where the two disagree: both put late August through mid‑September as the quietest stretch at both parks. They differ on early summer: crowd ratings drop sharply from May at Disneyland, while wait times fall more gradually. Sunday as Disneyland's quietest weekday comes from crowd ratings only and runs against the usual locals‑on‑weekends advice, so treat it as a lead rather than a certainty.

          Weather comes from the Open‑Meteo historical archive (open-meteo.com) for Anaheim, 2016–2025: ten years of daily records from ERA5 reanalysis, a blend of weather‑station and satellite observations. Rain chance is the share of days with at least 0.04 in, which skips trace drizzle. Each weekly figure rests on about 70 days, so expect some week‑to‑week noise; the monthly table is steadier.

          Time of day and ride closures come from Queue‑Times' per‑attraction history for 42 major rides (2023–2025; hourly figures are 2025). Overlay versions like Haunted Mansion Holiday are merged with the regular ride, and rides are only counted from when they opened (Toontown reopened March 2023; Tiana's Bayou Adventure opened November 2024). Seasonal events are typical windows from the 2025–2026 calendars published by Disney Tourist Blog (disneytouristblog.com), Attractions Magazine (attractionsmagazine.com) and MickeyVisit (mickeyvisit.com); the 2026 holiday season uses Disney's announced dates (Nov 13, 2026 to Jan 6, 2027, per FOX 11, foxla.com). Disney hadn't announced 2027 event dates as of Oct 24, 2026. Announced refurbishments (it's a small world Oct 30 to Nov 12, 2026; Autopia from Feb 1, 2027, with no reopening date yet) come from Disney Tourist Blog's refurbishment calendar, updated Oct 16, 2026, and are flagged on those dates on top of the usual monthly closure patterns. A ride closed for refurbishment on a date is greyed out and can't be picked for that day, and comes off the day's list if it was picked. Sunset times are 2025 values from Open‑Meteo.

          Plan a day uses each ride's 2024–2025 average wait by hour, scaled by that ride's month and day‑of‑week averages and by how busy the week runs against its month. Hours are live from ThemeParks.wiki when it covers the date (see Live park hours below), else Disney's published hours for that date where available (read from Disney's official calendar at disneyland.disney.go.com on Oct 24, 2026, covering Oct 24 to Dec 5, 2026, including the early closes for Oogie Boogie Bash), otherwise the 2025 schedule for the same week and weekday; weather is the 2016–2025 hourly average for that week. The schedule picks, at each step, the ride whose wait is lowest relative to what it will be later in the day, weighing in the walk to get there (a slider on each day sets how much shorter waits count against fewer steps; a minute of walking always counts at least as much as a minute in line), nudging outdoor rides away from hot or wet hours. On a light day (rides done 90 minutes or more before the end), it also lets a ride wait for a later hour when that saves at least 5 minutes in line or weather, and at least 5 minutes for each hour it waits, keeping that plan only if every ride and meal still fits. It counts the walk between any two rides from their map locations: the straight line stretched by a third for winding paths, at your group's walking speed (about 50 m a minute for Relaxed, seniors or small children; 65 for Normal, middle-aged adults; 80 for Go-go, young adults), up to 15% slower in the busiest weeks. Ride, restaurant and show locations are © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License, extracted on Oct 25, 2026 and built into the app (the park_places table); restaurants and shows are the middle of their building, and each ride has a queue entrance and an exit picked from the doors mapped on its building, so a walk runs from one ride's exit to the next ride's entrance. To choose between doors we used the ride locations published by ThemeParks.wiki as a reference, once, by hand; none of their data is built into the app and the app never contacts them. These picks are best guesses. The two park gates are our own estimate. A restaurant missing from the map falls back to a simple map of each land. On top of the walk it adds 10 minutes for shops, snacks and distractions (8 from the gate to the first ride), and looks one ride ahead so the plan doesn't zigzag across the park, rope drop 30 minutes before opening, and a relaxed pace on the stay‑all‑day plan: 45‑minute lunch and dinner, and a 20‑minute rest break roughly every 75 minutes. Both plans leave the park at the same time so they cover a similar day; whichever finishes its ride list first gets free time. The split‑day break goes in the hours where waits, heat and rain add up highest, between 11 am and 5 pm. Lunch and dinner are always eaten in the park, never during the hotel break, at the nearest quick-service place to where you are (on your way in from the gate after a break), and the next walk starts from there. You can also ask for lunch or dinner around a set hour (for the whole trip, or for one wizard day): the meal starts within about half an hour of it, and if it can't, the plan says so and has that meal whenever it fits best. A lunch or dinner you picked a restaurant for, or set a time for, is always kept: when your rides are done early (or you picked none), the plan stays in the park with free time until that meal instead of leaving, and a finish you set before it moves later until the meal is over (past the usual 2 hours if needed, never past close); on a split day it goes after the hotel break. Before a hotel break or a park hop the plan walks you back to the gate; a hop then adds the walk across the Esplanade and the tap-in line at the other park (about 15 minutes on a typical day, scaled for how busy it is; no bag check, since the Esplanade is inside security). A Park Hopper day tries every way of starting in either park (or the Starting park you set, which it never changes), hopping from 11 am on the half hour, at most twice, with or without a midday break, plus hop times just before and after a restaurant you picked in the other park; until that visit, the meal is not eaten anywhere else. It keeps the plan with the meals you want, then the most rides, then the earliest finish, then the least time in lines and walking (weighed by the slider, each hop counting as its walk and tap-in), then lets rides on a park visited twice move to the visit where they fit better, and says which park it starts in and why. A one-park day offers only that park's restaurants. Free time names the nearest show or character spot. You can set a start (rope drop, 10 am, 1 pm or your own time) and a finish (at close or your own time); the plan only uses those hours, and a day that starts after lunch or finishes by 4 pm has no midday break. When picked rides or a chosen meal time don't fit, the plan can give a little more time: a finish you set can move up to 2 hours later (never past close), or the midday break can get shorter (always keeping at least an hour at the hotel), in 15‑minute steps, whichever fits the most with the fewest extra minutes. The “Your day” box and the plan say what changed, and which rides still won't fit even then. The Walking times page (in the More menu) shows the walk between any two mapped spots at each pace and crowd level; these are estimates, and the park gates may add time for security and tap‑in. Rides above the shortest rider's height can still be picked but are flagged, with a Rider Switch reminder, and plans show every ride's height; minimum heights follow MickeyVisit's height list (mickeyvisit.com, updated Aug 27, 2025, checked Oct 25, 2026). These are estimates from averages: the day‑of hourly detail is behind a Queue‑Times login. The two permanent character meets (Royal Hall princesses and Mickey's House) plan like rides, using Queue‑Times waits, inside the hours they usually run (our estimate). The Tonight note at the end of each plan lists that day's fireworks and nighttime shows: Disney's own showtimes where its calendar has published them, otherwise our typical estimate (fireworks around 9:30 pm, Fantasmic! at 9 and 10:30, World of Color at 9 and 10:15 on later nights), labelled as an estimate.

          Ride attributes (land, indoor or outdoor, whether you'll get wet, ride length) were compiled by Claude from the official attraction listings. Lightning Lane eligibility and rules (22 Multi Pass rides; Rise of the Resistance and Radiator Springs Racers as Single Pass; one booking at a time, next after you use it or 2 hours after booking) follow MickeyVisit's 2026 guide (mickeyvisit.com). Single Rider lines follow Disney's single rider page (11 rides). The plan assumes about one Lightning Lane every 1¾ hours in the park (up to 7), used on the eligible rides with the longest expected waits, at about 10 minutes each and at least an hour apart, since you can hold only one booking at a time; real return times depend on availability that day. Hotel travel times for the Grand Californian, Disneyland Hotel and Pixar Place Hotel are the walk from an average room to each park's turnstiles along the footpaths mapped in OpenStreetMap (about 430 m to Disneyland and 290 m into California Adventure through the Grand Californian's own entrance; about 845 m from the Disneyland Hotel and 830 m from Pixar Place Hotel), at your group's pace, plus 1½ to 2½ minutes for elevators and hallways. Harbor Blvd hotels use about 15 minutes at Normal pace, and the ART shuttle about 40 minutes whatever the pace. You can override them with a custom time. Every time you come in from the hotel, the plan adds our estimate of the security and tap-in line (the security_waits table): by time of day, from about 8 minutes an hour before opening to 18 at opening and 8 in the evening on a typical day, times 0.7 on quiet days up to 1.4 on busy ones. Disney doesn't publish these, so they come from blog reports (Disney Tourist Blog, Disney Dining, Inside the Magic, MickeyVisit). Grand Californian guests use the hotel's own California Adventure entrance at Grizzly Peak (about 5 minutes less in line) when it's open, by our estimate from an hour before opening until close; for Disneyland they walk to the Esplanade. A ride Disney has announced as closed for refurbishment on that date is left out of the plan.

          Pick my park days scores every day of your stay for each park with the same factors and slider weights as Find your best weeks, using that day's published or typical hours, weekday crowd rating and adjusted ride waits. Early‑close party nights at California Adventure are penalized. It then tries every combination of days (and parks, for one‑park tickets) and picks the highest total. Day‑to‑day weather within one week is the same seasonal average, so differences between nearby days mostly come from weekday crowds, hours, party nights and closures.

          Ticket prices are Disney's dated 1‑day, 1‑park ticket prices (7 tiers, $104–$224 per adult), read from the official ticket calendar at disneyland.disney.go.com on Oct 23, 2026, and published through June 30, 2027. Multi‑day tickets ($335 for 2 days up to $520 for 5, per adult) cost the same on any dates, per Disney Tourist Blog's report of the October 2026 price change. Weeks and days without a published price count as average.

          Find your best weeks scores each week from 0 to 1 on each factor relative to the rest of the year, weights them by your sliders and rescales so the best matching week is 100. "Comfortable temperature" favors feels‑like highs near 74°F and penalizes 90°F days and sub‑45°F nights.

          Long weekends: US public holidays come from Nager.Date (date.nager.at, free, no key), checked in the background and kept in your browser for up to 30 days; offline they're worked out from the usual federal rules. A Monday or Friday holiday with its weekend, or a Tuesday or Thursday one with the day between, is a long weekend; Plan a day, the trip wizard's dates and day pages warn when your dates touch one, or a Southern California school break from the built-in holidays list.

          Live park hours: park hours for about the next month, and today's evening showtimes for the shows listed, come live from ThemeParks.wiki (api.themeparks.wiki, free, no key). They're checked in the background, kept in your browser for up to 12 hours and never built into the app, and hours that don't look right are ignored. Offline, if the check fails, or for later dates, the plan uses Disney's published calendar or typical hours instead; each plan says where its hours came from.

          Live forecast: for dates within the forecast range, the planners and the Next 15 days table use a live hourly forecast for Anaheim, fetched by your browser: Vaisala Xweather (xweather.com) if you enter your own API keys in Settings (stored only on this device), otherwise Open‑Meteo (open-meteo.com), which is free and needs no key. Feels‑like is the NWS heat index from temperature and humidity. Beyond the forecast range, or offline, the 10‑year averages are used.

          Holidays and school breaks are the actual dates for Nov 2026 to Oct 2027: federal holiday weekends, Thanksgiving week, and winter, Presidents' week, spring and summer breaks spanning the Orange County and Los Angeles district calendars (ActivityHero, activityhero.com; MomsLA, momsla.com). Spring break season runs Mar 13 to Apr 11, 2027, around Easter on Mar 28. In later years they shift by a few days. Park hours in the weekly charts are the 2025 schedule; day plans use published hours where available.
